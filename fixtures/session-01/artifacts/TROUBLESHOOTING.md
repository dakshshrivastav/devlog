## Incident 001: Intermittent brownout reset on relay actuation

- Level: hardware · Status: resolved [voice 14:02] [voice 14:09] [photo 2] [voice 14:14]
- Phase: Actuation testing
- Detected: 2026-10-08T14:02:11Z
- Resolved: 2026-10-08T14:14:02Z
- Telemetry target: 3.3 V logic rail

**Symptom.** The controller restarted every time the relay coil was de-energized. A 15 ms software debounce on the drive pin did not change the behaviour.

**Measurements.**

- 3.3 V rail minimum at coil dropout: 2.65 V [voice 14:09]
- 3.3 V rail steady state after the fix: 3.28 V [voice 14:14]

**Root cause.** The coil was switched without suppression, so its inductive kickback reached the shared 3.3 V rail and pulled it below the controller’s brownout threshold.

**Fix.** A 1N4007 diode was fitted antiparallel across the coil terminals.

**Verification.** The rail held 3.28 V across fifty consecutive actuations with no reset, and a second capture at the same probe position showed no sag.

**Bench rules learned.**

- Clamp an inductive load before the first actuation test — A coil switched from a logic rail needs its flyback diode fitted before power is applied, not after a reset is observed. [voice 14:11] [photo 2]
- A timing change cannot fix an electrical transient — Debounce addresses contact bounce on the input side; it does nothing about back-EMF on the output side. [voice 14:11] [commit c4e12a]
